## 二、英文稿 1 Introduction

Floods are among the world's most frequent and widespread natural disasters, posing serious threats to human life and socioeconomic activity (CRED and UNDRR, 2020). Satellite observations indicate that the proportion of the population exposed within observed inundated areas is increasing (Tellman et al., 2021). Timely information on flood extent and water depth is essential for emergency response and post-disaster assessment. Synthetic aperture radar (SAR) uses active microwave imaging to acquire surface information under cloudy and rainy conditions and at night, and has become an important data source for operational flood monitoring (Wagner et al., 2026).

Inundation extent and water depth provide complementary flood information. Extent identifies affected areas, while depth describes the degree of submergence within them. Together, they support risk assessments for people and assets. Assessments of risks to people and vehicles during the Zhengzhou floods, for example, combined inundation conditions with hydraulic instability conditions (Dong et al., 2022). These two types of information are obtained on different bases. Specular reflection from smooth open water generally reduces backscatter, providing a basis for identifying inundation extent (Amitrano et al., 2024). Depth estimation additionally incorporates terrain information. Terrain elevations at inundation boundaries serve as water-level references for constructing a water-surface elevation field within the extent, and depth is then calculated as the difference between water-surface and ground elevations (Cian et al., 2018). In this process, the identified extent determines both the spatial support and the boundary references for depth estimation.

Extensive research has been conducted on SAR-based flood water identification and flood change detection (Jiang et al., 2021; Lee and Li, 2026). Threshold segmentation is suited to areas with homogeneous land cover and strong contrast between water and land, and deep learning improves water identification in complex settings through multiscale features and context modeling (Du et al., 2026). Bi-temporal change detection compares pre-event and post-event observations to provide temporal information for identifying newly inundated extents (Giustarini et al., 2013). Open water typically exhibits low backscatter, while flooding beneath vegetation may increase backscatter through multiple scattering (Tsyganskaya et al., 2018). These scattering differences produce flood-induced changes of varying strength (Amitrano et al., 2024). Weak changes in scattered patches, narrow strips, and vegetated areas are easily missed, whereas arid sandy surfaces, building shadows, and layover in built-up areas exhibit low backscatter similar to water and are easily confused with inundation (Garg et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2025). Identifying newly inundated extents therefore requires consideration of both surface states and change responses at the two times.

Existing feature fusion and context modeling methods improve change separability (Saleh et al., 2024; Du et al., 2026), but the statistical comparability and local positional correspondence of bi-temporal features are not explicitly constrained in these designs. Differences in imaging conditions, speckle, and local positions cause difference responses to contain both observation differences and surface changes. Calibration with shared bi-temporal statistics provides a common comparison basis, and deformable sampling (Zhu et al., 2021) allows local positional correspondence to be adjusted. Statistical calibration reduces the influence of observation differences on change responses, and local alignment reduces apparent changes caused by misregistration. Jointly encoding pre-event states, post-event states, signed differences, and absolute differences combines surface appearance with the direction and magnitude of change. Harmonizing bi-temporal features and jointly organizing state and difference information are therefore important aspects of flood-change encoding.

Flood-extent reconstruction must preserve the regional completeness of contiguous inundation and the local shapes of narrow flooded strips and scattered standing water. Multiscale representations express scale differences by fusing features at different levels or introducing feature pyramids (Wu et al., 2022; Zhao et al., 2023; Tahermanesh et al., 2025), while feature-selection methods organize multiscale information through attention weighting or adaptive windows (Yadav et al., 2022; Zhu et al., 2025). Bi-temporal semantic interaction and deep change priors further provide regional context for feature fusion (Chen et al., 2022; Han et al., 2023b). Contiguous regions require context over larger areas, whereas narrow strips and scattered patches require high-resolution edge and positional information. Regional aggregation may smooth local variations, whereas detail preservation alone may fragment contiguous regions. Mamba and VMamba model long-range dependencies with computational complexity that scales linearly with sequence length (Gu and Dao, 2024; Liu et al., 2024), and ChangeMamba applies state-space modeling to bi-temporal feature interaction in remote sensing (Chen et al., 2024a). These studies provide a basis for regional context modeling, and flood-extent reconstruction requires a decoder that combines deep regional aggregation with shallow detail recovery.

Water-depth estimation methods mainly comprise hydrodynamic models, data-driven models, and boundary-elevation-constrained methods. Hydrodynamic models simulate flood evolution by solving the Saint-Venant equations or simplified hydraulic relationships and require detailed channel topography and roughness parameters (Teng et al., 2017). Data-driven models use machine learning to regress water depth from SAR imagery and auxiliary data, relying on measured depth samples for training (Chu et al., 2025). Boundary-elevation-constrained methods take flood extent and a DEM as inputs and are suited to rapid disaster assessment. Cian et al. estimated water levels from lidar terrain elevations at inundation margins (Cian et al., 2018). FwDET derives water depth from inundation extent and terrain (Cohen et al., 2018). Version 2.0 improves boundary identification and assigns water levels to interior pixels using the elevations of the nearest boundary pixels (Cohen et al., 2019). FLEXTH constrains water-level propagation using terrain conditions (Betterle and Salamon, 2024). RS-FloodXDepth and Yang et al. connect water-level references with hydrologically guided region growing and water segmentation, respectively (Tian et al., 2026; Yang et al., 2026). Constructing and propagating boundary elevation references are common elements of these methods. Cohen et al.'s analysis of FwDET v2.1 shows that depth estimates are relatively sensitive to boundary smoothing and filtering parameters when medium- and coarse-resolution DEMs are used (Cohen et al., 2022).

The input extent affects depth estimation through the locations and number of boundary samples and their connectivity structure. In their comparison of flood-processing chains, Travert et al. found that input flood maps and method hyperparameters were important factors affecting depth results (Travert et al., 2026). When the input comes from change detection, the boundary of the newly inundated extent may adjoin pre-existing water bodies, unchanged higher terrain, or detection errors, so boundary samples mix elevation references with different meanings, and the connectivity structure may differ from that of the actual water body. Change-detection masks contain omissions and false alarms along their boundaries, whereas boundary-elevation-based methods generally treat the input extent as a water body with reliable boundary references. When a single boundary elevation serves as the reference, local elevation errors can affect interior estimates through water-level assignment. Expressing boundary references as intervals and solving jointly with terrain lower bounds and within-component water-surface continuity helps use both boundary and interior terrain information. Change-detection research focuses on identifying and spatially reconstructing newly inundated areas, whereas boundary-elevation-based depth research focuses on water-level references and propagation within a given flood extent. Connecting these tasks requires an explicit common solution domain so that detection results directly determine the spatial support and boundary references for depth estimation, with input-extent comparison used to evaluate their influence on depth results.

To address these needs, we propose Detection-to-Depth Flood Mapping (DDFlood), a two-stage framework that maps newly inundated extent and water depth from bi-temporal SAR imagery within a shared spatial support. HarmoSSM generates the newly inundated extent in the detection stage. In the depth-estimation stage, Change-Constrained Flood Depth Estimation (CCDepth) combines the detected extent with a DEM to reconstruct the water surface and output water depth. The main contributions are as follows.

1. **Harmonized encoding and omni-scale reconstruction for flood change detection.** We design HarmoSSM with a Harmonized Change Encoder (HCE) and an Omni-Scale State-Space Decoder (OSSD). Within HCE, Harmonized Alignment (HA) calibrates features using shared bi-temporal statistics and adjusts local positional correspondence, while Change Query Interaction (CQI) jointly encodes pre-event states, post-event states, signed differences, and absolute differences. HCE provides comparable bi-temporal change representations for identifying weak changes. OSSD aggregates regional context in deep change features and recovers local detail in shallow features, combining both types of information to reconstruct newly inundated extents of different morphologies.

2. **Depth estimation constrained by the change support domain.** We construct CCDepth using the median and median absolute deviation of terrain elevations on both sides of the boundary to form water-level intervals, with inner-side terrain statistics providing the lower reference and outer-side terrain statistics providing the upper reference. Robust statistics and boundary weights reduce the influence of local fluctuations in boundary elevation on the water-surface solution. Soft interval constraints in the boundary band, hard interior terrain lower bounds, and a weighted sum of squared water-surface elevation differences between adjacent pixels jointly define the water-surface optimization problem. Water-surface elevations are solved jointly within connected components with sufficient boundary support, and water depth is then calculated pixelwise.

3. **Two-stage mapping with shared spatial support and input-extent comparison.** DDFlood uses the HarmoSSM-detected extent and the DEM together to determine the CCDepth solution domain and boundary references. Water surfaces are solved independently within eight-connected components, so detection errors affect depth through the solution domain, boundary samples, and connectivity structure of the components in which they occur. With the DEM, solver parameters, and depth-class thresholds fixed, an input-extent comparison between manually delineated reference extents and detected extents is conducted to analyze how detection errors propagate into depth classes and output coverage. The framework is evaluated on GF3 and LT-1B imagery from three flood events, and the depth estimates of CCDepth are validated with ICESat-2 reference depths from the 2022 Pakistan floods.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the study areas and data. Section 3 presents the DDFlood framework and the designs of HarmoSSM and CCDepth. Section 4 describes the experimental setup. Section 5 reports the results of change detection, depth estimation, and framework performance. Section 6 discusses the depth-constraint mechanisms, detection-error propagation, and limitations. Section 7 concludes the paper.

## 三、中文稿 1 引言

洪水是全球发生最频繁、影响范围最广的自然灾害之一，对人类生命和社会经济活动构成严重威胁（CRED and UNDRR, 2020）。卫星观测表明，观测淹没区内暴露人口的比例持续上升（Tellman et al., 2021）。及时获取洪水范围和水深信息是应急响应和灾后评估的重要基础。合成孔径雷达（SAR）以主动微波方式成像，能够在多云多雨条件下和夜间获取地表信息，已成为业务化洪水监测的重要数据源（Wagner et al., 2026）。

淹没范围和水深提供互补的洪水信息。范围确定受影响区域，水深描述区域内的淹没程度，两者共同支撑人员和资产的风险评估。例如，郑州洪水中人员和车辆的风险评估结合了淹没条件和水力失稳条件（Dong et al., 2022）。两类信息的获取依据不同。平静开阔水面的镜面反射通常降低后向散射，为识别淹没范围提供依据（Amitrano et al., 2024）。水深估算需要进一步引入地形信息。以淹没边界处的地形高程作为水位参考，在范围内构建水面高程场，再以水面高程和地面高程之差计算水深（Cian et al., 2018）。在这一过程中，识别得到的范围同时决定水深估算的空间支撑和边界参考。

已有大量研究开展了基于SAR的洪水水体识别和洪水变化检测（Jiang et al., 2021; Lee and Li, 2026）。阈值分割适用于地物类型均一和水陆对比强烈的区域，深度学习通过多尺度特征和上下文建模提升了复杂场景中的水体识别能力（Du et al., 2026）。双时相变化检测比较灾前和灾后观测，为识别新增淹没范围提供时间信息（Giustarini et al., 2013）。开阔水面通常呈现低后向散射，植被下淹没可能通过多次散射使后向散射增强（Tsyganskaya et al., 2018）。这些散射差异使洪水引起的变化强弱不一（Amitrano et al., 2024）。零散斑块、狭长条带和植被区的弱变化容易被遗漏。干旱沙地、建筑阴影和建成区叠掩呈现和水体相近的低后向散射，容易被误判为淹没（Garg et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2025）。识别新增淹没范围需要同时考虑两个时相的地表状态和变化响应。

现有特征融合和上下文建模方法提升了变化可分性（Saleh et al., 2024; Du et al., 2026），这些设计未显式约束双时相特征的统计可比性和局部位置对应。成像条件、相干斑和局部位置的差异使差异响应同时包含观测差异和地表变化。基于双时相共享统计量的校准提供共同的比较基础，可变形采样（Zhu et al., 2021）可以调整局部位置对应。统计校准降低观测差异对变化响应的影响，局部对齐减少配准偏差引起的伪变化。联合编码灾前状态、灾后状态、有向差和绝对差，可以结合地表外观和变化的方向和幅度。协调双时相特征以及联合组织状态和差异信息是洪水变化编码的重要内容。

洪水范围重建需要保持连片淹没的区域完整性，以及狭长淹没条带和零散积水的局部形状。多尺度表示通过融合不同层级特征或引入特征金字塔表达尺度差异（Wu et al., 2022; Zhao et al., 2023; Tahermanesh et al., 2025），特征选择方法通过注意力加权或自适应窗口组织多尺度信息（Yadav et al., 2022; Zhu et al., 2025）。双时相语义交互和深层变化先验进一步为特征融合提供区域上下文（Chen et al., 2022; Han et al., 2023b）。连片区域需要更大范围的上下文，狭长条带和零散斑块需要高分辨率的边缘和位置信息。仅进行区域聚合可能平滑局部变化，仅保持细节可能使连片区域破碎。Mamba和VMamba以随序列长度线性增长的计算复杂度建模长程依赖（Gu and Dao, 2024; Liu et al., 2024），ChangeMamba将状态空间建模应用于遥感双时相特征交互（Chen et al., 2024a）。这些研究为区域上下文建模提供了基础。洪水范围重建需要结合深层区域聚合和浅层细节恢复的解码器。

水深估算方法主要包括水动力模型、数据驱动模型和边界高程约束方法。水动力模型通过求解圣维南方程或简化水力关系模拟洪水演进，需要详细的河道地形和糙率参数（Teng et al., 2017）。数据驱动模型利用机器学习从SAR影像和辅助数据回归水深，依赖实测水深样本训练（Chu et al., 2025）。边界高程约束方法以洪水范围和DEM为输入，适用于快速灾情评估。Cian等利用淹没边缘的激光雷达地形高程估算水位（Cian et al., 2018）。FwDET基于淹没范围和地形推求水深（Cohen et al., 2018）。其2.0版本改进了边界识别，以最近边界像元的高程为内部像元赋予水位（Cohen et al., 2019）。FLEXTH利用地形条件约束水位传播（Betterle and Salamon, 2024）。RS-FloodXDepth和Yang等分别通过水文引导的区域生长和水体分割连接水位参考（Tian et al., 2026; Yang et al., 2026）。构建和传播边界高程参考是这些方法的共同要素。Cohen等对FwDET v2.1的分析表明，使用中等分辨率或粗分辨率DEM时，水深估算对边界平滑和滤波参数较为敏感（Cohen et al., 2022）。

输入范围通过边界样本的位置、数量和连通结构影响水深估算。Travert等比较洪水处理链后发现，输入洪水图和方法超参数是影响水深结果的重要因素（Travert et al., 2026）。当输入来自变化检测时，新增淹没范围的边界可能紧邻原有水体或未变化的较高地形，也可能由检测误差形成。边界样本由此混合了含义不同的高程参考，连通结构也可能不同于实际水体。变化检测掩膜的边界存在漏检和虚警，基于边界高程的方法通常将输入范围视为边界参考可靠的水体。以单一边界高程作为参考时，局部高程误差会通过水位赋值影响内部估算。将边界参考表达为区间，结合地形下界和分量内水面连续性联合求解，有助于同时利用边界和内部地形信息。变化检测研究关注新增淹没区的识别和空间重建，基于边界高程的水深研究关注给定洪水范围内水位参考的构建和传播。连接两类任务需要显式的共同求解域，使检测结果直接决定水深估算的空间支撑和边界参考，再通过输入范围对比评价检测结果对水深的影响。

针对上述需求，本文提出了检测至水深洪水制图框架（DDFlood）。该两阶段框架在共享空间支撑下由双时相SAR影像获取新增淹没范围和水深。检测阶段由HarmoSSM生成新增淹没范围。水深估算阶段由变化约束洪水水深估算方法（CCDepth）结合检测范围和DEM重建水面，输出水深。主要贡献如下。

1. **面向洪水变化检测的协调编码和全尺度重建。**设计了HarmoSSM，由协调变化编码器（HCE）和全尺度状态空间解码器（OSSD）组成。在HCE中，协调对齐模块（HA）利用双时相共享统计量校准特征，调整局部位置对应。变化查询交互模块（CQI）联合编码灾前状态、灾后状态、有向差和绝对差。HCE为弱变化识别提供可比的双时相变化表示。OSSD在深层变化特征中聚合区域上下文，在浅层特征中恢复局部细节，结合两类信息重建不同形态的新增淹没范围。

2. **受变化支撑域约束的水深估算。**构建了CCDepth，利用边界两侧地形高程的中位数和中位数绝对偏差构成水位区间。内侧地形统计量提供下参考，外侧地形统计量提供上参考。稳健统计量和边界权重降低边界高程局部波动对水面解的影响。边界带内的软区间约束、内部的硬地形下界以及相邻像元水面高程差的加权平方和共同定义水面优化问题。在边界支撑充分的连通分量内联合求解水面高程，再逐像元计算水深。

3. **共享空间支撑的两阶段制图和输入范围对比。**DDFlood利用HarmoSSM检测范围和DEM共同确定CCDepth的求解域和边界参考。水面在各八连通分量内独立求解，检测误差通过所在分量的求解域、边界样本和连通结构影响水深。在固定DEM、求解参数和水深等级阈值的条件下，开展了人工勾绘参考范围和检测范围的输入范围对比，分析检测误差向水深等级和输出覆盖的传递。在三次洪水事件的GF3和LT-1B影像上评价了该框架，利用2022年巴基斯坦洪水的ICESat-2参考水深验证了CCDepth的水深估算。

本文其余部分安排如下。第2节介绍研究区和数据。第3节阐述DDFlood框架以及HarmoSSM和CCDepth的设计。第4节说明实验设置。第5节报告变化检测、水深估算和框架性能的结果。第6节讨论水深约束机制、检测误差传递和局限。第7节总结全文。
