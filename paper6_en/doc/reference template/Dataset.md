# 4. Dataset

To evaluate the effectiveness and the transferability of our proposed framework, we collected four landslide datasets comprising Lushan dataset, Wenchuan dataset, Zhouqu dataset, and Taiwan dataset. These datasets cover landslides triggered by different factors and captured using diverse remote sensing sensors with varying spatial resolutions. A brief introduction of all the datasets is summarized in Table 1, wherein the Lushan landslide dataset is used for training and the rest, marked in gray, are used for testing. Detailed introduction of each landslide dataset is illustrated in the following subsections.

## 4.1. Lushan landslide dataset for training

The Lushan dataset comprises landslides triggered by the 2013 Lushan earthquake, with a magnitude of $7.0\ M_s$. This earthquake caused widespread damage and thousands of landslides in Sichuan Province (Xu, Xu, and Shyu 2015). As depicted in Figure 8, the landslides within this dataset are primarily located in regions characterized by sparse vegetation and extensive bare soil, often intersected by roads and forestry activity. The Lushan landslide dataset is utilized for training the landslide mapping model due to the considerable variation in the spectral characteristics of the different landslides and the complex distribution of background objects. These factors enhance the robustness of the constructed model, allowing it to effectively map landslides across various practical application scenarios. The landslide inventory for the Lushan dataset has been manually adapted by three experienced researchers through visual interpretation, based on inventories from the published work of Xu et al. (Xu, Xu, and Shyu 2015). This adaptation was essential to accommodate the different spatial resolutions of the remote sensing images we collected, compared to those used in the study of Xu et al. (Xu, Xu, and Shyu 2015).

# 4.2. Landslide datasets for evaluation

Referring to Table 1, the landslide datasets of Taiwan, Wenchuan, and Zhouqu are employed to evaluate the transferability of our proposed framework. The landslides of Wenchuan and Zhouqu datasets are triggered by earthquakes with different magnitudes, while that of Taiwan dataset were not associated with specific seismic events. The landslide inventory for the Wenchuan dataset was sourced and visually adapted from the published works of Huang et al. (Y. Huang et al. 2023), whereas the inventories for the Taiwan and Zhouqu datasets were visually interpreted by the same three experienced researchers, with supplementary data provided by high-resolution images from Google Earth.

The images of Taiwan and Wenchuan datasets were sourced from Google Earth, which includes mosaicked scenes from various sensors and resolutions, as shown in Figure 9(a,b). This adds to the realism and complexity of the evaluation conditions. The Zhouqu dataset (Figure 9(c)), captured by the GaoFen-2 satellite, offers $1\text{-m}$ spatial resolution and reflects typical conditions for operational remote sensing-based disaster monitoring.

It is important to note that the landslides in these evaluation datasets differ significantly from the Lushan dataset in both spectral content and spatial patterns. Landslides in the Taiwan and Wenchuan datasets are mainly located in dense forested areas, whereas Zhouqu's landslides are concentrated in sparsely vegetated, erosion-prone regions. These differences, combined with the diverse resolutions listed in Table 1, provide a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating the generalization capabilities of our model. The distinct imaging conditions, sensor types, and background complexities across these datasets serve as a rigorous validation for assessing real-world applicability.
